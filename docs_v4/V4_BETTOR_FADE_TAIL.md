# Individual bettor profitability and FADE/TAIL walk-forward (research branch `research/v4-bettors`)

Production is unchanged: `v4-dev` 8c003b8, engine sportsbook_v5.r2 (verified 2026-11-11). Nothing is deployed or
merged, and no API credits were used.

## Answer to the main question
**If we had identified the most and least profitable synthetic bettors before each week and followed or faded their
NEXT bets, we would NOT have made money.**

| Rule (selected from earlier weeks only) | NFL next-week result | CFB next-week result |
|---|---|---|
| Follow the top 10% of bettors | 236-242-10, ROI −2.5% | 289-317-8, ROI −6.8% |
| Fade the bottom 10% of bettors | 238-238-8, ROI −3.6% | 308-280-3, ROI −1.3% |
| Follow the top 15 bettors | 59-52-1, +6.6% (CI −9.8% to +24.4%) | 37-48-3, −16.0% |
| Fade the bottom 15 bettors | 47-47-1, −3.8% | 58-47-2, +9.4% (CI −12.2% to +29.6%) |

All bets are graded at the best observed real price at the time of each bettor's wager. Closing value is −1.6% to
−2.8% in every case, i.e. the vig.

**Verdict: NO EVIDENCE.** No group of bettors carries credible predictive information.

## Method
- **Data:** the full-season replay ledger of the production engine (bet-for-bet identical to production): NFL
  weeks 1–9 and CFB weeks 0–10.
  - 146,205 wagers from 17,181 persistent bettor IDs; 2,819 of the 20,000 never bet.
  - Sum of per-bettor P&L = ledger P&L (−$1,015,868.01 vs −$1,015,867.81, cent rounding).
  - The ledger itself was re-settled and reconciled against the production settlement ledger to the cent in
    `V4_REALISM_AUDIT.md`.
- **Two more random seeds** of the same engine, and the corrected V4r1 model, evaluated separately and never pooled.
- **Phase 1 (per bettor):** record, wagers, staked, net $, ROI, and **excess vs the price-implied expectation** (actual
  minus expected units at the market makers' no-vig fair at decision time; z-score). Broken down by:
  - sport, market, favourite/underdog/over/under, spread range, moneyline odds range;
  - timing, stake bucket and archetype.

  Files: `docs_v4/bettors/bettor_summary.csv.gz` (17,181 rows). The long breakdown is regenerated by
  `python -m lab_v4.bettor_study` (4 MB, not committed).
- **Phase 3 (walk-forward, mandatory):** for every cutoff week from week 3, groups are frozen from **completed earlier
  weeks only**, and their next week's bets are graded:
  - **tail** = same side; **fade** = the opposite side, at its own best real price and number (never assumed at the
    bettor's price);
  - pushes count as 0 and vig is included;
  - correlated bettors on the same game-market side count **once**.
- **30 group rules, fixed before evaluation:**
  - top/bottom 15 overall and top/bottom 10%;
  - top/bottom 15 within each of the 7 archetypes;
  - top/bottom 15 within each of 6 bet types (spread favourite/underdog, over/under, moneyline favourite/underdog).

  Ranking uses evidence (excess z, minimum 5 bets; 3 per bet type). Raw P&L and ROI rankings are reported as
  variants.
- **Multiple testing:** Benjamini–Hochberg FDR 10% across all 112 out-of-sample tests.

## Phase 1–2 findings: is there persistent bettor skill?
- **Empirical-Bayes between-bettor variance of excess return: τ² = 0.** Observed differences between bettors are
  fully explained by sampling noise, so the optimal shrinkage pulls every bettor's estimate to zero.
- **Repeatability:** each bettor's excess in odd weeks vs even weeks correlates at **−0.10 to +0.03** in every
  archetype. CFB sharps show +0.12 (n = 215), not significant after correction.
- **Extremes:** only 2.8% of bettors have |excess z| > 1.96, *fewer* than the 5% luck alone produces.
- **The historical top and bottom 20 are 38 high-stakes recreational customers and 2 sharps,** with 2–23 bets each
  (tables below). Big stakes plus few bets produce the extreme dollar results; their price-implied expectation was
  about −4% like everyone else.

## Phase 3–5 results (full tables: `V4_BETTOR_FADE_TAIL_TABLES.md`)
- **0 of 112 out-of-sample tests survive FDR correction** in production seed 0, seed 1 or seed 2, and in the
  corrected V4r1 model.
- **Selection bias is extreme.** Groups selected on, for example, a 0-68 training record go 10-5 next; groups at
  +80% training ROI regress to roughly −vig.
- **Across seeds,** the out-of-sample excess keeps the same sign in all three seeds for **27 of 112** tests, the 25%
  that chance gives.
- **Best-looking candidates** (all fail FDR; listed only to show why they are not signals):
  - *TAIL: top-15 CFB over bettors* — 17-4 out of sample, p = 0.003 (FDR needs ≤ 0.0009). Seeds: z +2.7 / −0.6 /
    +1.6. Closing value −0.5% (no price edge). **D: random.**
  - *TAIL: top-15 CFB spread underdog bettors* — 20-8, z +2.2 / +1.2 / +1.2 across seeds, closing value −0.4%. Every
    CFB spread-underdog bet in those weeks also beat its price expectation (+5.9%). This is a **2026 market outcome
    (underdogs covered), not bettor information. C: explained by market outcomes.**
  - *FADE: bottom-15 CFB casual bettors* — 60-45, z +1.6 / +0.5 / +0.6, closing value −2.4%. **D.**
- **Group consensus** (Phase 4, de-duplicated by game-market):
  - ≥ 3 of the best bettors agreeing (tail): 18-13, +16.2%, closing value **−4.6%**, z 0.9.
  - ≥ 3 of the worst bettors agreeing (fade): 12-15, −22.6%, z −1.2.
  - Best vs worst bettors on opposite sides (tail the best): 8-9-1.
  - Agreement does not strengthen anything.
- **Robustness:**
  - groups of 10 instead of 15, starting at week 5, ranking by net $ or by ROI, and removing the largest 1% of
    wagers all leave the conclusion unchanged;
  - the one cell that looks good in one variant (top-10 NFL, +21.9%) is −11.9% when ranked by net $.

## What the patterns are (A–D)
- **A. Genuine recurring behaviour: yes, but it is preference, not skill.** Customers keep their traits (favourite
  or underdog, over or under, team loyalty), so *what* they bet repeats. *Whether it wins* does not.
- **B. Mechanically created by the model's rules.** Underdog contrarians bet underdogs; V4 sharps bet when the book
  lags the reference books (and buy −EV prices, see the realism audit).
- **C. Explained by market outcomes.** 2026 side-type results move group records. NFL unders −14% vs expectation,
  NFL moneyline underdogs −13%, CFB moneyline underdogs −22%, CFB spread underdogs +5.9%. A group whose members
  prefer that side inherits the result, and so does anyone who simply bets that side type.
- **D. Random winners and losers.** Every individual top/bottom bettor and every group candidate.

**Why this was expected:** synthetic customers choose sides from fixed preferences and random draws. They never see
information about the outcome (only V4 sharps see prices, and their rule is mis-specified). A bettor's past results
therefore cannot carry information about future outcomes beyond the side type they prefer, and that is already
priced in or is a market pattern.

## Final verdict
| Candidate type | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Follow historically profitable bettors (any grouping) | **NO EVIDENCE** |
| Fade historically unprofitable bettors (any grouping) | **NO EVIDENCE** |
| Bet-type groups (e.g. CFB over / underdog bettors) | **NO EVIDENCE** of bettor-specific information. Any effect is the side type's 2026 market result. |
| Group consensus (≥ 3 agreeing) | **NO EVIDENCE** |

No candidate qualifies as PROMISING BUT UNPROVEN under the stated rules: nothing survives correction, nothing is
stable across seeds, and closing value is −vig throughout.
